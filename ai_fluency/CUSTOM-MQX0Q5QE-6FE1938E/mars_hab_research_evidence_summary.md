# MarsHab AI Literature Review — Research-Stage Evidence

**Purpose:** Evidence that **MarsVision AI: Intelligent Habitat Site Recommendation for Future Human Exploration of Mars** is a genuine next ML Work case study in an active research stage.

## Source and scope

This summary is based on Umer Sajid’s supplied workbook, `MarsHab_AI_Literature_Review_Expanded.xlsx`, reviewed on August 14, 2026. The workbook contains three worksheets: a project README, a literature-review table, and a research-gap matrix.

The literature-review table contains **20 populated research records** spanning Mars habitat/site suitability, terrain perception, remote-sensing inputs, AI methods, explainability, and human-settlement context. The raw workbook is intentionally not committed here; this public-safe summary records only its high-level project direction and evidence boundary.

The source workbook identifies these five project-relevant gaps:

| Observed gap | Potential MarsVision AI contribution | Future test direction |
|---|---|---|
| Habitat suitability studies are mainly multi-factor geospatial analysis rather than end-to-end explainable AI. | An explainable AI decision-support layer for site ranking. | Compare AI ranking with a transparent baseline. |
| Mars deep-learning studies focus mainly on rover navigation, trafficability, or geomorphology. | Connect terrain perception to habitat suitability. | Compare terrain-only, metadata-only, and fused approaches. |
| Visual terrain and environmental/resource factors are often separate. | A multimodal or staged-fusion approach. | Test image-only, metadata-only, and fused models. |
| AI predictions need meaningful uncertainty and explanations. | Calibrated confidence and explanations. | Use calibration metrics and qualitative review. |
| Cross-mission generalization is challenging. | Cross-mission evaluation. | Train on one source and test on another where feasible. |

## Honest current status

> MarsVision AI is at the **literature-review and problem-framing stage**. The research workbook is real evidence of that stage, but it is not evidence of a trained model, validated site-ranking system, public deployment, or completed result. Those claims will be made only after the relevant work and evaluation have actually been completed.
