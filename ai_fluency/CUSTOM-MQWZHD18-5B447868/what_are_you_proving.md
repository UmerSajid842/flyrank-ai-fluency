# What Are You Proving?

## Proof statement

I build **leakage-aware machine-learning prototypes** for content-prioritisation problems. I want an **entry-level machine-learning hiring manager at an SEO or content-intelligence company** to **open my capstone case study**. It shows how I used a public-safe, anonymised content dataset to rank pages for editorial review, held out entire pseudonymised clients to test transfer, excluded outcome-period signals to reduce leakage, and reported the result with its limitations instead of presenting the model as an automatic decision-maker. My evidence is specific: on the held-out split, the model achieved Precision@50 of 0.540 compared with 0.340 for a transparent baseline; the result is a measured ranking comparison, not a promise of traffic recovery.

> My portfolio needs to exist because a CV or LinkedIn profile can list Python and machine learning, but it cannot let a reviewer inspect the actual problem framing, leakage boundary, held-out evaluation, limitations, reproducibility path, and public-safe explanation behind those words.

## Claim, person, and action check

| Element | Decision | Why it is deliberately narrow |
|---|---|---|
| **One claim** | I build **leakage-aware machine-learning prototypes**. | The claim names one primary capability: applying leakage-aware ML judgment. It does not bundle a list of tools or unrelated skills. |
| **One person** | An entry-level ML hiring manager at an SEO or content-intelligence company. | This person can assess the capstone’s content-prioritisation problem and relevance to an ML role. |
| **One action** | Open the capstone case study. | The case study—not a generic contact page—is the strongest available evidence for the claim. |

## Evidence boundary

The capstone’s public paper uses aggregate results from a 30,000-page anonymised starter release across 32 pseudonymised clients. It does not publish client names, domains, URLs, page titles, private queries, raw exports, or page-level recommendations. The capstone explicitly frames its output as a ranked **review-for-refresh** queue for decision support, not causal proof or automated action. [1]
